Mission

Aid That Ends Because It Worked

The New Foreign Assistance works with Congress, the executive branch, and the policy community to shape the next generation of U.S. foreign assistance — leaner, evidence-based, and aligned with American interests.

Our model is South Korea: once dependent on American aid, today the world's tenth-largest economy and an aid donor. That graduation is the standard. Every program should be designed to make itself unnecessary.

Economic growth is the primary driver of development. The U.S. should fund what only governments can do — trade, institutions, infrastructure, security — and get out of the way of everything else.

Framework

Nine Principles for Reformed
Foreign Assistance

An evidence-based framework for what U.S. foreign assistance should look like — aligned with American interests, designed to produce results, and built to end.

01

Economic Growth As the Goal

Rising household incomes are the most efficient path to improving human welfare. The goal is trading partners and allies, not a global safety net.

02

Do What Only Governments Can Do

Trade agreements, regulatory reform, public health infrastructure, large commercial deals. Not household programs better handled by philanthropy.

03

Trade and Investment First

Where commercial solutions exist or can be developed, assistance should work through them. Investment over dependency, opportunity over aid.

04

Partner with Reformers

Prioritize countries that demonstrate commitment to reform. Deeper engagement is earned, not automatic. Poor governance is not America's responsibility to subsidize.

05

Measure Everything

Every program should have clear, measurable outcomes defined before it launches. Cost-effectiveness analysis should be the norm. If we can't measure it, we shouldn't fund it.

06

Finite by Design

The best foreign aid ends — because it worked. Built-in graduation criteria and transition plans. That graduation should be celebrated, not feared.

07

No Abortion Funding Abroad

Abortion is fiercely contested in the United States. It is inappropriate for the U.S. government to fund it overseas, or organizations that provide it alongside other services.

08

Rights Without Imperialism

Oppose the criminalization of LGBT individuals. But advocacy for particular social positions on sexuality and gender in sovereign nations is cultural neo-colonialism, not development.

09

Climate Realism

Foreign assistance is not an effective climate policy tool. The most effective climate resilience comes through economic growth and technology transfer — not energy restrictions on poor countries.

About
Daniel Handel
Founder & Executive Director
Policy Director, Unlock Aid
Former USAID Foreign Service Officer
Former Senior Specialist, 3ie

Daniel Handel spent eleven years as a USAID Foreign Service Officer, with field assignments in Rwanda and Nicaragua and Washington postings focused on aid effectiveness. While at USAID, he created the agency's first cash benchmarking initiative — testing whether traditional programs outperformed simply giving people money — work covered by NPR, the Washington Post, and the Center for Global Development.

He subsequently served as a senior evaluation specialist at the International Initiative for Impact Evaluation (3ie), where he helped establish the Global Evidence Commitment. He currently serves as Policy Director at Unlock Aid, working closely with State Department officials on foreign assistance reform, procurement innovation, and the DEALS Applied Policy Solicitation.

He writes on foreign assistance for National Affairs, Fox News, and The Hill.
